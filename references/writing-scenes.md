# Five Writing Scenes

For a formal or substantial deliverable, use the reader's main task to select one card if it fits; otherwise use the existing task route without a card. These are flexible content guides. Draft, Edit and Audit still determine what you may change; the medium does not determine the scene. A meeting note, email or slide can serve different tasks.

Before drafting or substantially restructuring, inspect the request, prior decisions, source and existing format. If it is unclear whether the reader needs to approve, act, assess evidence or do something else and that distinction changes the draft, ask one focused question before drafting the affected portion. For example: “Is this for the approving office to decide whether to extend the trial, or for participating units to act on its end date?” Reuse what is established. If a format is also unclear, use [manuscript format](manuscript-format.md); a confirmed scene does not supply a missing mandatory form. A question may resolve both only when they are the same decision.

When the user asks you to proceed without questions, do useful separable work. Name the unresolved distinction, or state the consequential assumption you actually used, and label any affected draft provisional. A provisional label alone does not tell the user what remains undecided. Skip scene intake for ordinary answers, small edits and literal transcription. Use the main reader task for mixed deliverables and apply another card locally where needed. Follow governing requirements over any suggested order; borrow only authorized features of a specimen. Reuse the [shared review](review-loop.md), including evidence, language, punctuation and table-number checks.

Apply the same source fidelity to every card. Omit operational details only when irrelevant, outside the required scope, and safe to omit without changing the remaining meaning. If a rule appears as background in a notice, request for approval, application or other draft, keep who may, must or must not act, and under what conditions. For example, a source that requires manual review without retry can be reported as “标记为人工复核的记录转人工复核，不得重试”; “无需重试” weakens that rule. If the source merely says a step is unnecessary, do not upgrade that to a ban.

When stating a rule, keep its actual activity and object explicit. For example, given “试点周五结束。继续推广须经批准”, use “试点周五结束；后续推广须经批准” if both facts matter. “试点周五结束；继续开展须经批准” could make the rule apply to continued pilot operation, which the source did not establish. If only the end date matters and dropping the rollout condition does not mislead, give the end date alone. A proposal to extend the pilot remains a proposal, even when reported next to the rollout condition.

## Check the delivered text against the source

Within the shared review, make a brief source-to-delivery comparison for material facts and rules in a scene deliverable based on supplied material. Privately identify what the reader needs and the task requires. For each affected statement, check the action and object, executor and decision maker, time and status, and force and conditions, where relevant. Start with the final wording when checking support; start with the source and task when checking for omissions. Omit only details the task allows you to omit without changing meaning.

- When reporting a settled schedule beside a proposed change, keep each status attached to its own action. For example, “现行窗口定于周三关闭；现申请延长至周五” preserves both. “拟于周三关闭” makes the settled schedule tentative; “已延长至周五” invents approval or implementation.
- Read each standalone action card without the prompt, cover message or nearby document. Name the executor in the relevant row or assign that role within the card. For example, “登记员须将材料交审核员，由审核员决定” states both roles. “须交审核员，由审核员决定” does not identify the executor on its own. Keep permissions and prohibitions attached to their actual actors and conditions.
- For each handover row, compare the executor, action and applicability conditions with the source. Read its label together with the action: a label can limit who may act or when. If the source gives no prerequisite, use a neutral action label in the required column, such as “查询状态”. For “文员可查询状态”, a row headed “已提交” adds a condition; “查询状态｜文员可查询状态” does not. Retain actual source conditions on other rows. Forwarding a record for review does not establish who performs that review.

Use a concise check inside the same round. Do not add a public ledger or change the confirmed headings. Ordinary short answers and literal transcription do not acquire a scene intake or new workflow.

## 1. Official documents

- **When and reader task:** Identify the actual issuing and receiving bodies and the act. A notice tells recipients what to know or do; a request for approval presents a decision for the competent authority; a report informs; minutes record what the meeting decided. Use [official-document review](official-document-review.md) where applicable. Apply Party and government form only where it governs; other organizations may use their own approved forms.
- **Minimum content and order:** Orient the recipient, then provide the decision or action, its basis, and any relevant scope, responsibility, time limit or attachments. When only a notice body will be delivered, state its established audience or scope in the body; do not rely on an omitted address line or title. In a request for approval, put the specific approval sought where the reader can find it. In minutes, separate discussion, adopted decisions and open items.
- **Do not infer:** Authority, signed approval, official document number, effective date or responsibilities. Do not put an approval request into a report where the governing rules prohibit it.
- **Acceptance:** Can the recipient identify the intended act, who may decide or must act, and when? Check the applicable genre, actual form and supporting source.

## 2. Academic writing

- **When and reader task:** The reader needs to assess an argument, research method or finding. Identify study type, manuscript section, venue and applicable reporting guidance. Use [academic review](academic-review.md). An original empirical paper can often use Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion; use the appropriate organization for the actual section, review type or thesis.
- **Minimum content and order:** Use the requested section's function. Make the research question, evidence, warranted interpretation and limitations findable where applicable. For results, report observed outcomes and their scope without importing discussion. Check applicable reporting items separately from submission layout.
- **Do not infer:** Unreported analyses, statistical significance, causation, participant counts, unavailable citations or methods not performed.
- **Acceptance:** Can a reader locate the evidence, tell what was actually done, and distinguish a result from its interpretation? Check the venue's confirmed requirements.

## 3. Engineering and technical writing

- **When and reader task:** Determine whether the reader needs to execute or take over an operation, understand a design, look up an interface, review an engineering decision, or learn from an incident. These uses need different sequences.
- **Minimum content and order:** For operation and handover, establish prerequisites and authority, then give steps, verification and supported exception, stop, rollback or escalation paths. For design decisions, explain context, alternatives, chosen or proposed option, consequences and status. For incident review, distinguish impact, timeline, observations, causal assessment and follow-up. Teach or provide lookup material only to the extent the task calls for it.
- **Do not infer:** Execution, permission, test outcomes, unverified causes or an undocumented recovery step. In a handover, distinguish what the recipient may do from what they must do. State only source-supported status/action pairs: a status label such as “accepted” does not by itself establish that an action is unnecessary. Leave a needed route pending if it is not supplied. A decision marked proposed does not authorize implementation.
- **Acceptance:** Can the operator act in success, failure and unknown states that the source distinguishes? Can a reviewer find the supporting evidence, decision status or interface constraint needed for the task?

## 4. Research and decision support

- **When and reader task:** The reader needs to understand findings or choose a course. Clarify whether the deliverable reports a study or investigation, or asks a decision maker to act.
- **Minimum content and order:** For research findings, give the question, coverage and method, supported findings and limits. For a decision brief, state the decision needed, hard constraints, options and a common basis for comparison. Include the status quo when it is a useful baseline. State the supported recommendation, key uncertainty and what would change it.
- **Do not infer:** Comparability, a causal explanation, an approved decision or certainty from estimates. Do not hide a failed hard constraint behind a favorable score.
- **Acceptance:** Can the reader identify the evidence for each material finding and, for a decision, say which option meets the criteria and why? Trace any material table totals back to their sources.

## 5. Project applications

- **When and reader task:** A sponsor or internal reviewer needs to decide whether to support proposed work. Identify the call, sponsor or approving body, year, applicable form and assessment criteria. Use confirmed requirements and inspect the current form when required.
- **Minimum content and order:** Use the prescribed order, or arrange the supported case around the need, proposed work and outcomes, feasibility or prior foundation, and any applicable deliverables, resources and evaluation basis. Distinguish achievements from plans and estimates.
- **Do not infer:** Eligibility, partners, funding, approvals, commitments, completed tests, resources or a template field. If the current required form is unavailable, do not call a provisional draft ready to submit.
- **Acceptance:** Can the reviewer explain what is requested, why the project is worth doing and what supports feasibility? Verify the actual submission requirements separately.
