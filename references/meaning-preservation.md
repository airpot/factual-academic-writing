# Meaning Preservation

Use these shared safeguards in all modes. A light edit can keep the comparison in working context.
For a substantial rewrite, keep a compact record of high-risk meanings. Reuse existing author or claim records.

## New composition and source fidelity

In a new answer, correct the assistant's own errors, including an error carried from earlier conversation.
Do not preserve an incorrect assertion merely for continuity. Explain a material correction when the user needs it to understand the result.
Distinguish established facts, interpretations, assumptions and recommendations. Keep verification appropriate to the task and its source limits.

In expression-only editing, the source remains the meaning target. Preserve its assertions and report proposed evidence corrections separately.
Apply substantive corrections only when the task authorizes them. Do not add an unprovided premise to make the source argument appear complete.
In an audit, assess support and propose corrections without treating that assessment as permission to rewrite the source.

A clearly hypothetical teaching example can introduce invented participants or values to explain a concept.
Requested fiction can introduce imagined events. Keep the fictional frame and supplied creative constraints clear.
Do not present either as observed evidence, a real source or a completed execution. Hypothetical content does not authorize invented nonfiction claims.

## Shared safeguards

- Invent no factual evidence, findings, references, mechanisms, methods or author decisions. Keep hypothetical and fictional content within its stated frame.
- Keep actors, quantities, units, dates, negation, conditions, exceptions, scope and logical relations.
- Preserve direct quotations, including internal punctuation. Do not silently shorten them or move punctuation.
- Keep source identifiers, code, formulas and established terms unless their correction is requested.
- Keep the author's research question and supplied judgment. A proposed reading is not established author intent.
- Keep correlation, causation, sequence and parallel facts distinct. Bind credit to the documented action. “The volunteers built the set” leaves who designed it open; “the set was their idea” adds a different claim. Do not make that shift through a possessive, metaphor or introductory phrase.
- Preserve commitment in both directions. Do not make possible actions completed or definite capacities conjectural.
- Keep each distinct supplied substantive limit in expression editing. Combine only duplicate meanings with the same clear scope.

## Identify protected meanings

Record only fields needed for this task. Not every claim needs every field.

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Actor and action state | Who did, can do, prepared, intends or might do what? |
| Commitment | Is this an observation, capacity, possibility, interpretation or proposal? |
| Condition and scope | Under which condition, population, period or exception does the statement hold? |
| Quantity basis | Which numerator, denominator, unit, overlap, input version and rounding apply? |
| Relation and judgment | What links evidence to the claim, and what position does the author take? |
| Protected wording | Which quotations, definitions, terms, code or formulas must remain exact? |

In expression-only work, the original is the meaning target, even when its evidence is questionable.
Report a supported evidence correction separately. Apply it only when the task includes evidence correction.
In drafting and evidence audits, distinguish source observations, author interpretations and agent proposals with the evidence model.

## Compare in both directions

1. From revision to material: identify the basis of each substantive assertion, including premises, relative clauses and background comparisons.
   A supplied new capability does not establish what the old workflow lacked.
   Missing information in supplied material does not establish that a study omitted the procedure or measurement.
2. From material to revision: locate each required meaning, author judgment and distinct limit in the revised text.
   Check for omissions disguised as concise expression or improved factual precision.
3. Check relations after splitting or reordering. A leading possibility marker does not qualify every later clause automatically.
4. Read the whole revised artifact. Check that restored meanings fit its argument, referents and requested voice.

For a local repair, insert the proposed words into the original sentence and read the resulting passage. Check what the usable repair itself says about the actor, observation or inference, timing and author stance or person. If a fragment fixes the syntax but drops a material fact, propose the smallest span that preserves both. Keep facts in the revised prose even when the diagnosis already names them. Compare who did what, under which condition, and with what consequence. “The swatches stayed in the wash tank for an hour” does not preserve “we rinsed the swatches for an hour”; the tank names the setting, not the action. Check whether splitting or joining clauses changes who is bound. “The coordinator logs requests; no one may erase a pending request” preserves a general ban. “The coordinator logs requests and must not erase a pending request” states a ban only for the coordinator. If both duties actually belong to the same role, preserve that scope too. A proposed replacement for a particular result should retain the required group-to-condition pairing in that result, unless the task permits replacing the surrounding passage as a unit. Preserve a material absence expressly stated within a source's scope: “The register has no renewal date for this permit” differs from “The register cannot prove the permit was renewed.” The former describes the register and does not establish that renewal never happened. If the available source merely cannot settle renewal or the register's contents, do not claim the date is absent.

When the deliverable is meant to state a supplied rule, check the trigger in both directions. “When a label is replaced, we check it again” says what happens in that case but does not limit the requirement to that case. If only replacement triggers a required check, write “We need to check the label again only if it is replaced.” This keeps the author's person and the rule's exclusive scope without claiming that voluntary checks never occur. In an expression-only edit of an inconsistent source, report the discrepancy separately if changing the rule is outside scope.

Example: the supplied note says the team checked a timer and confirmed a reset at 11:00. In “After checking the timer, the reset was at 11:00,” the opening phrase lacks its actor. “After the team checked the timer, the reset was at 11:00” fixes attachment but leaves the timing as a bare assertion. In a first-person team note, “We checked the timer and confirmed that the reset occurred at 11:00” preserves the evidence relation and voice if the source supports that account.

## Interim wording in an audit

Separate the confirmed clause from the unresolved one. Ask the author exactly what would settle the unresolved part. If two or more references can resolve differently, name each and ask for an answer for each: “Which team does ‘they’ refer to in the opening and in the caption, respectively?” A single shared question is enough when the source establishes that the references are linked. Do not ask about a recoverable reference or turn an unresolved assignment into an instruction. For alternative cutoff schedules, test before, at and after the boundary only where those cases affect action. If a separate instruction leaves the exact instant disputed, name the date or occasion and ask whether that instant is included under the affected schedule. Do not infer permission from the absence of a cutoff on that day or infer a ban where none is established. A clearly specified inclusive or exclusive cutoff needs no question. If proposals are in scope and the remaining statement is useful and faithful, propose it locally and label what remains pending; do not claim to have edited the source. For example, if a log confirms that both teams received notice but does not identify which requested a deferral, propose “Both teams received notice” for the confirmed part and ask who requested the deferral. Where the request itself is confirmed and matters, retain it separately without attribution. If separating the claims would change what is known, ask and defer the change.

## Preserve the scope of qualifications

A source's uncertain explanation can have a definite consequence if that explanation holds.
Keep uncertainty at the explanation. Preserve the stated conditional consequence without adding uncertainty to it.
When the consequence itself is possible, preserve that separate possibility too.
Possible confounders remain possible. A limitation's premise needs the same care as the main result.
An asserted capacity remains a capacity. Preparation, a request, an imagined action and completion remain different states.
Distinguish asserted capacity from hypothetical capacity. Preserve the source's contextual force, such as “can” versus hypothetical “could”.
“The key could fit in the envelope” must not become “the key is small enough to fit” when the fit remains hypothetical.
Check abstract restatements too. Wording about size or readiness can silently strengthen a possibility without using an explicit completion verb.

## Compact comparison example

Synthetic material: a library page lists required identification before collection.
The author states that this page can inform readers before a visit.
Some readers may miss the message. The library has prepared a printed notice, but has not posted it.
No prior notification process is supplied.

| Proposed wording | Decision and basis |
| --- | --- |
| The page moved notification from the desk to the home. | Revise. The old notification location is not supplied. |
| The page might inform readers before a visit. | Revise. This weakens the asserted capacity into conjecture. |
| The library posted a printed notice. | Revise. Preparation is not posting. |
| The page can inform readers before a visit, although some may miss the message. | Retain. Capacity and possible non-use remain distinct. |

Before calling the passage complete, also retain preparation of the notice if that fact serves the requested argument.
Supporting every surviving sentence is insufficient when a required meaning has disappeared.

## Numerical and quotation checks

For calculations, retain inputs, denominator, units, assumptions, source versions and relevant rounding.
A percentage-point difference is not a relative percentage change. Different samples do not establish individual change.
Arithmetic alone establishes neither significance nor causation. Keep justified statistical conclusions and their supplied method and scope.
Exact string comparison can help check quotations or fixed identifiers. Review the context and attribution separately.
Use appropriate mechanical checks when available. They do not establish source truth or research quality.

For prose-only output, express preserved uncertainty in the claim. Do not narrate this checking procedure in the manuscript.

## Verify table numbers before delivery

Use this check in Draft, Edit and Audit whenever a table-derived number, its unit or its classification could change a factual claim, comparison or conclusion.

1. Within the allowed source scope, inspect the applicable original table first, including its sheet, date or version, and unit notes where needed. Use a readable screenshot or extract when the original is unavailable; describe what was actually inspected if that limit matters. A conflicting transcription does not override an inspected original. If applicability remains in dispute, leave the affected item pending.
2. For each material claim, record the source row or record key and column. Verify the value, sign where relevant, effective unit and category or inclusion rule. Check header, row and footnote scope. A timestamp alone may not distinguish rows; use other available fields or mark identity pending. Establish which records belong in a subtotal before adding.
3. Convert like quantities to a common unit. Use an available calculator, spreadsheet or code tool to recompute each material total from verified inputs. Follow the governing rule for which values to use, at what precision, and whether rounding happens before or after aggregation. Do not use a draft total or display-rounded values in place of the required operands. Keep the table and applicable version or sheet, cell locators, operands, conversions and result privately. If a rule governs rounding or requires exact amounts, record the rule and its source (for example, a provision, table note or confirmed task instruction), the decisive facts and their source locators, and the actual order of operations. Tie the facts to the records or period they cover. If more than one rule appears to govern the same calculation and they conflict, record the controlling basis for precedence and its source locator; do not infer priority from a later date or greater specificity alone. If a necessary calculation or governing basis cannot be verified, keep the affected result pending.
4. After the last edit, start with the delivered wording and trace every material table-derived figure, inclusion claim and dependent conclusion back to its source cells and, when applicable, the calculation. Verify the recorded rule and its source, the evidence of applicability, any precedence basis and its source, and the actual order of operations. If a material value, unit, record identity, category, rule applicability or precedence cannot be verified, locate what is pending and withhold only the dependent figures or conclusions from confirmed findings. Preserve independently verified findings. For expression-only edits, preserve the author's assertion in the edited copy and flag the evidence gap or factual correction separately; do not present that assertion as verified. An audit does not authorize rewriting the source.

### Worked unit examples

- **Wrong:** “The total is 50,002.4 万元” adds the visible numbers 2.4 and 50,000. **Correct:** In a refunds table headed 万元, row F1 has 2.4 with no override and F2 expressly has 50,000 元. Verify both rows belong in the subtotal, convert F2 to 5 万元, and recompute: the total is 7.4 万元.
- **Wrong:** Infer that a 3 万元 threshold was met solely because two amounts display as 1.50 万元 each. **Correct:** If the original cells say 1.4999 万元 and 14,999 元 and the rule uses exact amounts, the total is 29,998 元, or 2 元 below the threshold. If the governing rule instead rounds each line to 0.01 万元 before summing, the operands are both 1.50 万元 and the total meets the threshold exactly. Use the applicable rule; display rounding alone establishes neither result.
- **Wrong:** Read a clipped unit as 万元 to complete the subtotal. **Correct:** Confirm the fully legible row separately, name the row whose unit is unreadable, and leave the combined subtotal and dependent comparison pending.
- **Wrong:** Apply a pilot's per-line rounding because its memo is newer than the standard rule. **Correct:** If the pilot memo expressly overrides the standard for enrolled batches during its effective period, retain the override provision and the enrollment entry that identifies this batch and period. Check both against the actual rounding order before reporting the threshold outcome. If enrollment or the override is unresolved, report verified inputs and leave the dependent outcome pending.
